Why Your Breaker Keeps Tripping: When to Call an Electrician

By Beatrice Pino, Owner, Apex General Contracting, Inc. (CSLB #1072290) | Published August 12, 2026 | Updated August 12, 2026

A circuit breaker trips to protect your home. It cuts power before wires can overheat. When the same breaker trips over and over, treat it as a warning. Electrical distribution problems play a role in an estimated 51,000 U.S. home fires each year, with roughly 500 deaths and $1.3 billion in property damage, according to the Electrical Safety Foundation International (ESFI).

Five causes account for most repeat tripping: an overloaded circuit, a short circuit, a ground fault, a failing appliance, or a worn-out breaker. You can check some of these yourself in ten minutes. The others need a licensed electrician.

Reset It Once, the Right Way

Flip the breaker fully OFF until it clicks, then back ON. If it holds, you had a one-time overload, such as the microwave and toaster sharing a circuit.

If it trips again within minutes or hours, a real fault exists. Reset a breaker one time only. Each reset on an unresolved fault pushes another current surge through the problem the breaker keeps trying to stop.

The 5 Causes of a Tripping Breaker

1. Overloaded Circuit (Most Common)

The circuit carries more devices than it was built for. The classic Fresno case: a 1960s or 1970s home wired with 15-amp circuits, now running a window AC unit, a 65-inch TV, and a gaming PC. Symptoms include tripping when a specific appliance starts, or lights that dim when the AC compressor kicks on.

Safe check: Unplug a few devices and move them to outlets on a different breaker. If the tripping stops, you found the cause. The lasting fix is often a dedicated circuit for the heavy load.

2. Short Circuit

A hot wire touches a neutral wire, and current takes a shortcut the wire cannot handle. Signs include a burning smell near an outlet, scorch marks, or a breaker that trips the instant you reset it. Shorts start inside devices, cords, and walls (a nail through a wire, rodent damage).

An instant re-trip means stop resetting. Leave this repair to a licensed electrician.

3. Ground Fault

The hot wire finds a path to ground, often through moisture. Kitchens, bathrooms, garages, and outdoor outlets require GFCI protection for this reason. A GFCI that keeps tripping points to a wet outlet, an appliance with internal moisture, or deteriorated outdoor wiring.

4. A Failing Appliance

Sometimes one dying device causes the trips. Run the isolation test: unplug everything on the circuit, reset the breaker, then plug devices back in one at a time with a few minutes between each. When the breaker trips right after you plug in the space heater, you have your answer.

5. A Worn-Out Breaker or Failing Panel

Breakers wear out. Years of heat cycles in a Fresno garage or attic weaken the internal mechanism until it trips below its rated load. A licensed electrician can swap a breaker in minutes. Tripping across multiple breakers is different: it points to a failing bus bar or an aging panel. Panels last 25 to 40 years. Past that age, or if you have a Federal Pacific or Zinsco panel, plan for replacement.

Why Breakers Trip More in Fresno Summers

At 105°F, your AC runs long cycles and draws maximum current. Dust and cottonwood clog the condenser and raise that draw further. Circuits that coasted through spring hit their limits in August, and a window unit in an older home can push them past. A breaker that only trips during heat waves still warns you: the circuit has no headroom.

If your home predates AFCIs, repeated tripping is one more reason to have an electrician inspect the system.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a tripping breaker dangerous?

The trip is the safety system working. The cause is where the danger lives: overloads, shorts, and ground faults generate heat where heat does not belong. Repeated tripping is the symptom. Fix the cause.

How many times can I safely reset a breaker?

One. If it trips again, the fault still exists. Repeated resets force current through an unresolved problem and can damage the breaker, the wiring, or the wall cavity around them.

Why does the breaker trip when I run the microwave and toaster together?

Both draw heavy current, and many Fresno kitchens put them on one 15- or 20-amp circuit. Run one appliance at a time, or have an electrician add a dedicated kitchen circuit. It is a common, affordable upgrade.

Can I replace a breaker myself?

We advise against it. Even with the main breaker off, the service lugs inside the panel stay live with lethal current, and an arc flash happens faster than you can react. A licensed electrician replaces a breaker in minutes and tests the circuit at the same time.
