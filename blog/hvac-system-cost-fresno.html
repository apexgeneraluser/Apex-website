How Much Does a New HVAC System Cost in Fresno, CA? [2026]

By the Apex Air, Heating & Electric Team · 2026 · Fresno, CA

If you're pricing out a new HVAC system in Fresno, the honest answer is: it depends. But homeowners deserve real numbers, not just "call for a quote." Here's what full installs are actually running in the Central Valley this year, and why.

Typical Installed Cost Ranges in Fresno

These are all-in installed prices — equipment, labor, permits, and standard ductwork connections — for a typical Fresno-area single-family home. Unusual duct work, electrical panel upgrades, or attic access issues can push costs higher.

System TypeTypical Installed Range
Central AC only (existing furnace stays)$9,000 – $19,000
Furnace only (existing AC stays)$5,120 – $11,200
Full AC + furnace system$16,000 – $30,000
Heat pump (all-electric, heat + cool)$15,000 – $32,000
Ductless mini-split (single zone)$9,100 – $19,500
Why the range is so wide A 1,400 sq ft ranch home in Clovis and a 3,200 sq ft two-story in Fig Garden don't need the same tonnage, ductwork, or labor time — and neither of those looks like a Tower District bungalow with old, undersized ducts. Any quote worth trusting starts with a load calculation, not a square-footage guess.

What Actually Drives Fresno HVAC Pricing

  • System size (tonnage). Bigger homes, high ceilings, and lots of west-facing glass need more capacity — and more capacity costs more.
  • Efficiency rating. Higher SEER2 systems cost more upfront but run cheaper through a Fresno summer where the AC can run 12+ hours a day for weeks at a time.
  • Ductwork condition. Homes built before the 1990s often have undersized or leaky ducts. Sealing or replacing them adds cost but is often what actually fixes uneven cooling.
  • Electrical panel capacity. Heat pumps and higher-efficiency systems sometimes require panel or circuit upgrades, especially in older homes around Tower District, Fig Garden, or downtown.
  • Permits and Title 24 compliance. California requires HERS testing and permits for most HVAC replacements — a legitimate installer builds this into your quote rather than skipping it.
  • Attic and access difficulty. Tight attic crawl spaces, roof-mounted units, or second-story installs add labor time.

Ways to Bring the Cost Down

  • PG&E and manufacturer rebates. High-efficiency heat pumps and AC units frequently qualify for utility or federal tax incentives — ask your installer what's currently active before you sign anything.
  • Financing. Most Fresno HVAC companies, Apex included, offer 0% or low-interest financing plans that spread the cost over 12–120 months.
  • Bundling repairs. If your ducts, thermostat, or electrical also need attention, doing it during the same install visit is almost always cheaper than a separate trip.
  • Right-sizing instead of upsizing. A correctly sized system that runs full cycles is cheaper to buy and run than an oversized one that short-cycles and wears out early.

Getting a Number You Can Trust

Phone quotes and square-footage rules of thumb are a good way to get a surprise later. A trustworthy Fresno HVAC company will walk your home, measure your existing ductwork, check your electrical panel, and run a real load calculation (a Manual J, in industry terms) before giving you a firm number — not a range that balloons once the crew shows up.
