Critical Safety Notice

Do You Have a Federal Pacific or Zinsco Panel?

If your home was constructed between 1950 and 1990, inspect your electrical panel branding immediately. Millions of homes were equipped with panels manufactured by Federal Pacific Electric (FPE Stab-Lok) or Zinsco / GTE Sylvania.

Federal Pacific (FPE Stab-Lok)

In independent tests conducted by fire investigators and the CPSC, FPE breakers failed to trip in up to 60% of overcurrent conditions. When breakers fail to trip, wiring overheats inside your walls, leading to electrical fires. Most California insurance companies refuse to insure homes with FPE panels.

Zinsco & Sylvania Panels

Zinsco panels feature aluminum bus bars that corrode and melt. Breakers frequently weld themselves to the bus bar, making it impossible for the breaker to trip or be manually turned off. Buzzing sounds or scorching on Zinsco panels indicate imminent electrical failure.

Why should I upgrade my electrical panel to 200 amps?
Homes built before 1990 typically have 100-amp service panels designed when household power demands were a fraction of today's loads. Adding high-draw modern equipment—such as a Level 2 electric vehicle charger, an electric heat pump or new 4-ton AC, solar, or an ADU—will quickly exceed 100-amp capacity, causing frequent breaker trips and overheating. Upgrading to 200 amps future-proofs your home and eliminates fire risks.
Are Federal Pacific and Zinsco electrical panels dangerous?
Yes. Independent forensic testing demonstrated that Federal Pacific (FPE Stab-Lok) and Zinsco breaker panels suffer from alarmingly high failure rates, frequently failing to trip during overloads or short circuits. When a breaker fails to trip, current continues to flow through overloaded wiring, causing residential fires. Most California insurance companies refuse to issue coverage on homes with these panels until they are upgraded.
